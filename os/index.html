The master prompt

Add it to every prompt. AI asks before it guesses, rates its own work and tells you where your thinking is weak.

Before you start:
Restate my goal in one line, and tell me if it’s the right goal.
Don’t assume. If you need more context, ask me up to 3 questions first.

When you finish:
Rate your work out of 10. Say what would make it a 10, then fix it.
Push back on me: where is my thinking weak, what could go wrong, what am I missing?
Tell me the one thing to do next.
Tell me what you learned about me or my work that should be saved to my Context page, so you’re smarter next time.

Recurring work audit

Run it monthly. It finds what to turn into a skill, what to schedule and what to stop doing.

You are my Chief of Staff. Audit everything I do on a repeating basis and tell me what to turn into a skill, what to turn into a scheduled task, and what to stop doing.

Look back over the last 60 days in: Gmail (sent mail and templates), Google Calendar, Slack, Notion, Granola meeting notes and Google Drive. Count something as recurring if it happened 3 or more times, or happens on a fixed rhythm (daily, weekly, monthly, per client or per event).

Before you start:
Restate my goal in one line, and tell me if it’s the right goal.
Don’t assume. If you need more context, ask me up to 3 questions first.

When you finish:
Rate your work out of 10. Say what would make it a 10, then fix it.
Push back on me: where is my thinking weak, what could go wrong, what am I missing?
Tell me the one thing to do next.
Tell me what you learned about me or my work that should be saved to my Context page, so you’re smarter next time.

Sort every item by department:
Sales: outreach, follow-ups, proposals, CRM updates, pitches
Marketing and Content: newsletter, LinkedIn, Instagram, repurposing, event promo
Operations: admin, scheduling, invoicing, onboarding, team handoffs
Programs: delivery, workshops, client work

Use these definitions:
Skill: a repeatable task I trigger on demand that needs my context, voice or judgement. Example: writing a follow-up email after a sales call.
Scheduled task: something that runs on its own at a set time and doesn’t need me to start it. Example: a Monday 8am pipeline brief.
Both: a skill that a scheduled task runs on a timer.
Cut: work that doesn’t move my top goals forward.

For each item, give me one table row with these columns:
Department | Task | How often | Hours per month (estimate) | Evidence (link to email, thread or doc) | Skill, Scheduled task, Both or Cut | Why, in one line | Trigger or schedule

Then give me:
The top 5 items ranked by hours saved per month.
For the number one item, a draft skill description (name, when it triggers, inputs, output) or a scheduled task spec (name, cadence, time, what it delivers and where).
Any email I send repeatedly that should become a template. Quote the first line of each.

Rules:
Evidence only. If you can’t link to it, leave it out.
Don’t create, send or schedule anything. This is an audit.
Keep the whole thing to one page. Put the table first and skip the intro.
If a source isn’t connected, say which one in a single line and carry on with the rest.

Save it once

Set a text shortcut so typing ;mp pastes the master prompt. Text shortcuts handle line breaks badly, so use the one-paragraph version above.

  • Mac: System Settings → Keyboard → Text Replacements → +. Replace: ;mp. With: the paragraph. Click Add.
  • iPhone: Settings → General → Keyboard → Text Replacement → +. Phrase: the paragraph. Shortcut: ;mp. Save. If you set it up on your Mac first, iCloud syncs it.
  • Test it: open Notes, type ;mp and press space.
