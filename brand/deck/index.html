01 — What changed in v5.0

Fluro is out.
Pastel green is in.

Same brand, calmer signal. The acid lime fought the deep green and burned the eye on every slide. Pastel green does the same job and lets the dark green be the expensive thing in the room.

Retired

Fluro lime #D4FF4F

Off the brand. Anywhere it survives — old slides, old exports, old graphics — swap it.

Now

Pastel green #CCEEA0

The single accent. The stop, the CTA, the one highlighted word. Nothing else.

Promoted

Deep green #2C3422

The primary ground. Not near-black any more — the brand is green, top to bottom.

02 — The essence

What's the point of AI if it doesn't buy your life back?

Time Rich is the education and community that teaches women to delegate the shit they hate to AI — and run their business and their life on it. Application-based, because the quality of the room is the whole point.

Mission

Close the gap. Women adopt AI about 25% less than men. We make implementation normal, not intimidating.

Belief

AI doesn't save you time unless you set it up right. Context beats prompts. Build lean. Stay independent. Stay human.

Difference

No gurus. No bros. No growth-at-all-costs. No 12-week curriculum. Just the room, and women who ship.

03 — Who it's for

The room is the product

Everything we make is aimed at one person, and deliberately not at the other. If a draft would land with the right-hand column, it's off-brand.

For
  • Women and queer founders, investors and operators
  • People who've already built something with a proven offer
  • Builders who want to implement this week, not study it this year
  • Founders staying independent and lean
Not for
  • People looking for motivation, inspiration or awareness
  • Anyone who wants a cohort, curriculum or graduation
  • Growth-at-all-costs, VC-trap thinking
  • Corporate speak and empowerment language

06 — Logo rules

The stop is a signature, not a texture

One per composition. Use it after a headline, as a bullet, or as the cursor on a loading state — then stop.

Do
  • Cream on deep green, deep green on cream. Nothing else.
  • Clear space of one cap-height all round. Minimum 40px tall.
  • Set it live in Space Grotesk Bold — it's type, not a file.
  • Square mark for avatars: the lone stop, or TR.
Don't
  • No purple and no fluro. Plum and acid lime are both retired.
  • No stretch, skew, shadow, gradient or outline.
  • No sage stop. Sage is a background, never the mark.
  • Don't re-draw it. If it isn't Space Grotesk, it isn't the brand.

09 — Colour · the three laws

Pastel green is a fill, not text on cream

On deep green it can be text, the stop and the accent. On cream it is a fill only — a pill, a button, a highlighter swipe. Any green text on cream drops to moss.

10 — Typography

Space Grotesk speaks. Mono keeps the receipts.

Display
Buy it back.

Space Grotesk Bold, tight tracking, sentence case. Headlines and the wordmark.

Structure
> 21+ HOURS / WEEK

JetBrains Mono, uppercase, letter-spaced. Labels, data, tags, the terminal voice.

Body

Short sentences land harder. Then a longer one carries the argument.

System sans. Fast, native, readable. Never for headlines.

The stack, exactly: Space Grotesk display · system-ui body · JetBrains Mono labels and data. No serif. Stats as digits: 25%, never "a quarter."

11 — Buttons & CTAs

Three buttons, one job each

Pastel green for the one action that matters, an outline for the second option, a bare text link for the rest. Every button names what happens next and points with a →.

Apply to join → Come to a workshop → Read the playbooks
Do
  • One primary per screen. If everything's pastel, nothing is.
  • Always the → on primary and secondary.
Don't
  • Never "Learn more" or "Get started." Name the action.
  • No pastel label on cream. Fill stays pastel, text goes deep green.

12 — Tone of voice · the four traits

Sound like a founder, not a brand deck

Direct, warm, slightly impatient with nonsense, and a little cheeky. If you only remember one slide, remember this one.

01

Direct

The point lands in sentence one. No warm-up, no throat-clearing, no build to the reveal.

02

Warm

Founder to founder, not brand to audience. We assume she's competent and short on time.

03

Specific

Numbers, named tasks, real before-and-after. "21 hours a week," never "more time."

04

A little cheeky

"Delegate the shit you hate." Dry and confident. Never mean, never trying too hard.

TIME RICH.15 / 23

13 — Tone of voice · what we never are

Four ways a draft dies

×

Corporate

No empower, leverage, unlock, ecosystem, seamless, synergy. Say the actual thing.

×

Hyped

No game-changing, transformative, 10x, groundbreaking. No exclamation marks, ever.

×

Scarce

No spots filling fast, no don't miss out. Confidence, not scarcity. The room sells itself.

×

Vague

No hedging, no passive voice hiding the subject, no "things." Find the noun.

The one test: read it out loud. If it sounds like something Ella would say to one founder over coffee, it ships. If it sounds like a brand deck, a LinkedIn thought-leader, or ChatGPT with the safety on, it doesn't.

TIME RICH.16 / 23

14 — The voice firewall

Binary. One break and the draft fails.

Non-negotiables
  • No em-dashes or double dashes as connectors. Rewrite the sentence.
  • No exclamation marks anywhere.
  • No hedging: kind of, sort of, perhaps, might possibly.
  • No fake urgency: limited time, spots filling fast, don't miss out.
  • No promotional inflation: transformative, groundbreaking.
  • No passive voice hiding the subject. Say who did it.
  • No "thing" or "things." Find the actual noun.
Banned → replace
Never writeWrite instead
game-changingsay what it changes
empowersay what they can now do
leverageuse
unlockopen, access, be specific
seamlesssmooth, or describe it
ecosystemcommunity, network
thing / thingsfind the noun

Sign off everything except Substack with Warmly, Ella. Never "Ella x," "Cheers," "Talk soon," or "Best,".
