Relationships and the client journey

Capture every contact, run every client the same way, and stay in touch without remembering to.

 3 prompts
Contact capture Open in Claude 
Build me a simple contact system for people I meet at events, workshops and communities. Give me the columns (name, where we met, date, what they need, next step, follow-up date) and a 2-line follow-up message I can send within 48 hours. My business: [what you do and who you serve].
Client journey map Open in Claude 
Map my client journey step by step: discovery call, proposal, contract, delivery, report, review and follow-up. For each step give me what happens, what the client receives, and a short email template. My service: [your main offer and price].
Check-in schedule Open in Claude 
Write a check-in sequence for past clients at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months and 1 year. Each message under 80 words, warm, no hard sell, and gives them a reason to reply. Keep past clients connected even if they don't buy again. My service: [what you deliver].

Sales, partnerships and speaking

Find the right people, reach out like a human, and get booked to speak.

 4 prompts
Prospect and partner list Open in Claude 
Research 20 potential clients and 20 referral partners for my business in [city]. Partners can include [e.g. realtors, designers, architects, private members clubs]. For each: name, why they fit, who to contact, and one personal opening line. My ideal client: [describe them].
Outreach and follow-up Open in Claude 
Write a 3-touch outreach sequence for [type of prospect]: a first message, a follow-up after 4 days, and a final nudge after 10 days. Short, specific, one clear ask. My offer: [what you do and the result you get people].
Podcast and speaking pitch Open in Claude 
Find 10 podcasts and 5 event series where my audience listens, with the host name and why I fit. Then write a pitch under 120 words with 3 talk titles. My expertise: [your topic]. My audience: [who they are].
Workshop for a venue Open in Claude 
Create a 45-minute workshop outline I can pitch to [venue, e.g. design showrooms, members clubs]. Include the title, the promise, 5 sections, one hands-on moment, and a short pitch email to the venue's events lead. My topic: [your topic].

Website and client materials

A site that sells, offers that convert, and materials clients keep.

 4 prompts
Website and packages Open in Claude 
Review my website [URL] as my ideal client would. Then rewrite my homepage headline, 3 pricing packages (name, what's included, price), and the booking and checkout steps so it takes 3 clicks or fewer to buy. My ideal client: [describe them].
Emotional hooks Open in Claude 
My clients come to me wanting [e.g. wealth, love, calm]. For each intention write a headline, a 2-line description and a call to action I can use on my site and in posts. Sound like a person, not a brochure.
Email list magnet Open in Claude 
Create a one-page checklist my ideal client would swap their email for. Title, 7 to 10 practical tips, and a closing line that points to my offer. Then write the 3-email welcome sequence that follows. My topic: [your topic].
Client questionnaire and report Open in Claude 
Create a client intake questionnaire (10 questions max) and a report template I fill in after the session: summary, findings, recommendations, next steps. Make it feel premium and easy to complete. My service: [what you deliver].

Business support

Hand off the admin and get a second brain for big decisions.

 3 prompts
Admin assistant Open in Claude 
Here are the admin tasks I repeat every week: [list them]. Sort them into automate, delegate to AI, or keep. For each AI task, write the exact instructions I can give an assistant so it runs without me.
Business coach Open in Claude 
Act as my business coach. Ask me 5 questions, one at a time, about my revenue, offers and time. Then give me the 3 moves that would make the biggest difference in the next 30 days, in order.
Grants research Open in Claude 
Find grants, competitions and funding programmes I could apply for as a [type of business] in [country/city]. For each: name, amount, deadline, eligibility and a link. Flag the 3 best fits.

The Superhuman AI Dictionary

36 AI words you'll hear in the program, explained like a smart friend would over coffee. Each one has a picture to remember it by and one thing to try this week.

The basics

AI model (or LLM)

What it is: the brain behind Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. LLM stands for large language model: it learned from a huge pile of text and predicts the best next words.

Think of it like: a brilliant new hire who has read the whole internet but has never met your customers.

Try it: ask the same question in two models and see which one sounds more like you.

Prompt

What it is: what you ask the AI to do.

Think of it like: ordering at a restaurant. "Food please" gets you anything. "Grilled salmon, no butter, extra greens" gets you dinner.

Try it: say who it's for, what good looks like and what to avoid, every time.

Master prompt

What it is: the short checklist you add to the end of any prompt so the AI asks before guessing, rates its own work and pushes back.

Think of it like: the "measure twice, cut once" sign on a carpenter's wall.

Try it: type xx after your prompt (your shortcut from Week 1).

Context

What it is: everything the AI knows about you and your business when it answers.

Think of it like: briefing a babysitter. No brief, and the kids get ice cream for dinner.

Try it: point your AI at your Business COMPASS before any task.

Context window

What it is: how much the AI can hold in its head in one chat.

Think of it like: a desk. Pile on too many papers and the early ones fall off the back.

Try it: when a chat gets long and the answers get worse, start a fresh chat.

Hallucination

What it is: when the AI makes something up and says it with total confidence.

Think of it like: the friend who gives directions to a restaurant that closed in 2019.

Try it: add "if you don't know, write Needs input". Check every number and name before it goes out.

Token

What it is: the small chunks of words the AI reads and writes. Usage limits are counted in tokens.

Think of it like: coins in an arcade. Long chats and big files spend more coins.

Try it: upload the one page that matters, not your whole Drive.

Your setup

Personal preferences

What it is: standing instructions your AI reads before every chat: who you are and how you like answers.

Think of it like: your coffee order the barista already knows.

Try it: paste your master prompt in here once.

Project

What it is: a room in your AI with its own instructions and files, for one area of your business.

Think of it like: separate notebooks for sales, content and ops, instead of one messy one.

Try it: run every sales prompt in your Sales Floor project.

Memory

What it is: what your AI remembers about you between chats.

Think of it like: a colleague who remembers your kid's name. Lovely, until it remembers the wrong detail.

Try it: check what it remembers in settings, and keep sensitive topics off.

Connector

What it is: a bridge that lets your AI read (and sometimes write to) your tools: Notion, Gmail, your calendar.

Think of it like: giving your assistant a key to the filing cabinet, not the whole house.

Try it: ask "Can you see my Notion and my Gmail?" before a session.

MCP

What it is: the plug standard most connectors use (Model Context Protocol).

Think of it like: USB-C for AI. One shape of plug, lots of tools.

Try it: you don't need to build one. Just know it's why new connectors keep appearing.

Business COMPASS

What it is: the one page that tells your AI who you are, who you serve, what you sell and your current goal.

Think of it like: your business's passport. No passport, no entry.

Try it: update it whenever your offer or price changes.

Voice dictation

What it is: speaking instead of typing (Wispr Flow in this program).

Think of it like: leaving a voice note for your business instead of writing it an essay.

Try it: speak your next prompt. You'll give three times the context in half the time.

Inside your AI

Chat

What it is: one conversation with your AI. It remembers what's said in that chat, not in your other chats.

Think of it like: a phone call. Hang up, and the next call starts fresh.

Try it: one chat per job: one for your offer, one for this week's posts.

Artifact

What it is: something finished your AI builds next to the chat: a doc, deck, page or small tool you can edit, share or download.

Think of it like: the chat is the kitchen, the artifact is the plate that goes out.

Try it: ask for your client onboarding checklist "as an artifact".

Project instructions

What it is: the standing rules every chat inside a project follows.

Think of it like: the house rules on the fridge.

Try it: paste your Sales Floor rules once, instead of in every prompt.

Web search

What it is: your AI looks something up online and shows you where it found it.

Think of it like: a friend who checks before answering, and shows you the receipt.

Try it: ask for today's prices on a tool you use, with sources, then click one.

Research

What it is: a longer search in several steps that comes back as a report with sources.

Think of it like: sending an intern to the library for the afternoon instead of asking one quick question.

Try it: a report on 5 competitors' offers and prices.

Browser agent (Claude in Chrome)

What it is: AI that works inside your web browser, reading pages and clicking for you.

Think of it like: lending someone your laptop while you watch over their shoulder.

Try it: let it copy your event sign-ups into a sheet, and check before it submits anything.

Plugin

What it is: a ready-made bundle of skills and connectors for one role.

Think of it like: a starter kit instead of buying every tool one by one.

Try it: look for one that fits your business before you build your own.

Styles

What it is: presets that change how your AI writes: tone, length and format.

Think of it like: choosing the outfit for your words.

Try it: make a style from 5 of your best posts.

Getting work done

Skill

What it is: a saved set of steps your AI runs by name, so you explain it once, not every week.

Think of it like: a recipe card. Same dish, every time, whoever's cooking.

Try it: turn the task you repeat most into a skill on Friday.

SOP

What it is: standard operating procedure: the same steps written for a person.

Think of it like: the skill's twin, for humans.

Try it: ask your AI for the SOP version of every skill you save.

Workflow

What it is: a chain of steps that gets one job done, start to finish.

Think of it like: a relay race. Each runner hands the baton to the next.

Try it: write your client onboarding as steps before you automate any of it.

Automation

What it is: a workflow that runs on a trigger, the same way every time ("when a form comes in, add a row").

Think of it like: a light on a timer. Great at one job, no opinions.

Try it: automate the step you do exactly the same every time.

Agent

What it is: AI that can plan, use your tools and take several steps toward a goal, deciding the order itself.

Think of it like: the difference between a light on a timer and an intern with a to-do list.

Try it: give an agent one clear goal and the rule "drafts only".

AI intern

What it is: our name for an agent with a job, like your Sales Floor intern.

Think of it like: a keen intern: fast, tireless, needs a good brief and someone to check the work.

Try it: brief it with Done, Know, When.

Scheduled task

What it is: a prompt your AI runs on its own at a set time.

Think of it like: an alarm clock that also makes the coffee.

Try it: a Monday 8am pipeline review.

Human in the loop

What it is: a person approves before anything goes out.

Think of it like: the AI writes the card, you sign it.

Try it: every prompt in this program says "drafts only, never send". Keep it that way.

Guardrails

What it is: the rules you give your AI about what it must never do.

Think of it like: bumpers at the bowling alley.

Try it: "Never invent a name, number or reason. Never send. Never delete."

Words you'll hear around the room

Multi-LLM

What it is: using more than one AI model and picking the best one for each job.

Think of it like: a toolbox, not one hammer.

Try it: draft in one, fact-check in another.

Knowledge base

What it is: the files and pages your AI can search for answers (your COMPASS, your SOPs, past proposals).

Think of it like: giving it the company handbook instead of letting it guess.

Try it: add your best three proposals to your Sales Floor project.

Fine-tuning

What it is: retraining a model on your own data. Expensive and almost never needed for a founder.

Think of it like: sending your new hire back to university when a good brief would do.

Try it: don't. Better context gets you 95% of the way.

Vibe coding

What it is: building an app or website by describing it to AI in plain words.

Think of it like: telling an architect what you want and watching the house appear.

Try it: describe one tiny tool you wish existed, like a client intake form.

AI slop

What it is: generic AI content that sounds like everyone else.

Think of it like: beige.

Try it: give the AI your real stories and words, and cut anything you'd never say out loud.

Questions

Yes for prompts and your Notion context. Skills and scheduled agents run in Claude, which is what the sessions teach.

Nothing is lost. Every session is recorded, and the recording appears on the Curriculum page, under the session it belongs to.

If you know in advance, send your question to the WhatsApp group or to emc@timerichclub.com and it gets answered on the call.

Where are the recordings?

On the Curriculum page, under each session. A Watch recording button appears on the session once the recording is up, usually within a day.

Recordings stay available for the whole program.
